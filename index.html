Case studies

CX Buyer Research: which features reach the most buyers

B2B SaaS · Staff UX Researcher · Quantitative survey

Result: Six features would reach about 83% of CX decision makers, with diminishing returns beyond that.

Question
To reach its customer growth goal, Service Hub leadership needed to attract new customers while keeping existing ones. What drives purchase and satisfaction among decision makers at small to corporate-size businesses that buy and use CX software, and which combinations of features would most engage them and set Service Hub apart from competitors?
Method
A 15-minute online survey with a MaxDiff exercise to rank the features buyers value most. The MaxDiff results then fed a TURF (Total Unduplicated Reach & Frequency) analysis, which identified the feature combinations that would appeal to the most decision makers.
Why this method
The survey built on earlier qualitative understanding to measure buyer needs at scale. Including a target, non-customer sample reduced bias toward existing customers.
Recruitment & ethics
1,009 decision makers who use customer service or customer success software, at B2B and B2B/B2C companies with 1 to 2,000 employees in Service Hub’s target industries, across North America, EMEA, and JAPAC. Non-customer respondents were recruited through a third-party sample vendor, and the survey was hosted in Qualtrics.
Analysis
Evidence
TURF accounts for overlap, so the best set is not simply the individually top-rated features.
Six features reached about 83% of decision makers. Beyond six, reach keeps rising, but not significantly.
Outcome

Identified the specific feature drivers that attract and retain customers across the bi-modal (upmarket and downmarket) spectrum, supporting product-market fit. The priority features help CX teams make strategic decisions, work efficiently, and focus on more complex customer needs. Findings informed roadmap priorities and go-to-market messaging.

CX Leader Research: uncovering leaders’ needs and product expectations

B2B SaaS · Staff UX Researcher · Qualitative interviews

Result: A research-based prototype whose elements went into the generally available (GA) product.

Question
Who is the new CX leader who oversees both customer support and customer success, and what are their evolving needs, priorities, and challenges?
Method
13 one-on-one, 60-minute in-depth interviews with CX leaders at B2B SaaS and IT companies: 6 from mid-market companies (26 to 200 employees) and 7 from corporate companies (201 to 2,000 employees).
Why this method
In-depth interviews suit an emerging role that is still taking shape, where the goal is to understand priorities and challenges in leaders’ own words.
How AI was used
Used AI to support qualitative analysis and coding of the interviews, then turned the insights into a working Lovable prototype.

Outcome
Design and engineering teams took direct elements of the prototype into the generally available (GA) product; see the public Customer Success Management page.

Strategic Synthesis: positioning Service Hub to win upmarket

B2B SaaS · Staff UX Researcher · Research synthesis

Result: Leadership used the synthesis to reframe Hub-level priorities for winning upmarket.

Context
HubSpot aspired to be world class at delivering value upmarket through multi-hub and Enterprise adoption. Product and GTM teams were craving insight on how to win upmarket, so I completed a strategic analysis of how to move upmarket and position Service Hub as a viable CX solution.
Question
The synthesis addressed 3 core questions:
  1. Despite being in the CX conversation, Service Hub isn’t leading it. Why?
  2. What must Service Hub do to demonstrate product market fit to Enterprise organizations?
  3. How does Service Hub capture more share of the upmarket TAM?
Method
Compiled existing primary research, syndicated studies, product usage data, and Sales roadblock data (why deals fell through) into an in-depth analysis of where and how Service Hub should compete upmarket. Used a Service Hub NotebookLM I built to identify primary research sources, and Glean to search the UX repository for related work.
Why this approach
HubSpot holds a wealth of data and insight, but it is hard for teams to absorb it all. Connecting Service Hub research and data directly to company-wide strategy made clear what it takes for the product line to win. The approach also met the need for urgency and alignment on winning upmarket as AI shifts the market.
From the readout
Outcome
Gave product leadership and go-to-market teams a clearer path forward through direct readouts and a Service Hub-wide presentation. Leadership used the insights to reframe Hub-level priorities for winning upmarket.

Research & Analytics Playbook: scaling research across product development

Program · Owens Corning · Stage-Gate new product development · Enabling others

Result: A research playbook that scaled research across Stage-Gate product development and became training material for new product managers.

Question
How can research be democratized and scaled across Stage-Gate product development, especially in the early stages, so teams get the right research at the right time?
Why a playbook
Limited research bandwidth meant not every project could be supported. When product managers ran their own research, scope was inconsistent from stage to stage, and research was often requested too late, in the later stages where primary research was crucial.
Approach
Built a playbook for the Stage-Gate process that helps each project team decide what type of research, if any, it needs, and gives them the tools to act on that decision.
What it covered
Which type of research, if any, a project needs
Templates for each type of research
Estimated timing and budget
Decision criteria to answer to move forward at each gate
Outcome
The playbook standardized research practices so research was faster and more relevant. It became training material for new product management hires and a resource in an internal talent development program.
