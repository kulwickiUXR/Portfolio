Case studies

Customer Experience (CX) Buyer Research: which features reach the most buyers

B2B SaaS · Staff UX Researcher · Quantitative survey

Result: Findings reprioritized a roadmap feature, shaped how AI features were packaged, and informed product-led growth.

Context
HubSpot Service Hub was on a mission to get X paying customers by 2028. To achieve this goal, the product line needs to add new customers, while also retaining and growing with the existing install base. As such, the research objectives were to understand the experiences of small to corporate size business decision makers when purchasing and using CX software, and to identify optimal combinations of features (current roadmap items and distinct competitor features) that will most engage purchasers and help to differentiate the product from competitors.
Method
A 15-minute, blind, online survey with MaxDiff and TURF (Total Unduplicated Reach and Frequency) analysis.
Why this method
The survey built on earlier qualitative understanding to measure buyer needs at scale. Including a target, non-customer sample reduced bias toward existing customers.
Recruitment & ethics
1,009 decision makers who use customer service or customer success software, at B2B and B2B/B2C companies with 1 to 2,000 employees in Service Hub’s target industries, across North America, EMEA, and JAPAC. Non-customer respondents were recruited through a third-party sample vendor, and the survey was hosted in Qualtrics.
Analysis

TURF accounts for overlap in interest, so the best set of features is not simply the individually top-rated ones. Six features reached about 83% of decision makers, and beyond six, reach keeps rising but not significantly.

Outcome
  • Identified two features, X and Y, as most valued by CX buyers, regardless of company size. As a result, the product leadership team reprioritized X, and it was brought to market sooner than planned.
  • MaxDiff results debunked an internal hypothesis about AI feature value. As a result, my packaging recommendations were used by go-to-market teams when deciding how much to emphasize the AI features available in the product.
  • Results were also used to inform upgrade motions and product-led growth opportunities in the app.

Customer Experience (CX) Leader Research

B2B SaaS · Staff UX Researcher · Qualitative interviews

Result: A research-based prototype whose elements went into the generally available (GA) product.

Context
Anecdotal evidence from CSAT verbatims, sales team feedback, and product usage data began to suggest disparate HubSpot Service Hub workspaces were not conducive for an emerging persona, CX Leader. This research was commissioned to understand the evolving needs, priorities, and challenges of upmarket CX leaders who oversee both customer support and customer success, and to translate those insights into product development.
Method
13 one-on-one, 60-minute in-depth interviews with CX leaders at B2B SaaS and IT companies: 6 from mid-market companies (26 to 200 employees) and 7 from corporate companies (201 to 2,000 employees).
Why this method
In-depth interviews suffice because the goal was to delve deep into an emerging role, understand priorities and challenges in the target persona’s own words.
How AI was used
Used Claude Code to code and analyze interview transcripts, and turned the insights into a working prototype, via Lovable.

Outcome
Design and engineering teams took direct elements of the prototype into the generally available (GA) product; see the public Customer Success Management page.

Strategic Synthesis: positioning Service Hub to win upmarket

B2B SaaS · Staff UX Researcher · Research synthesis

Result: Leadership used the synthesis to reframe product-line priorities for winning upmarket.

Context

HubSpot Service Hub was on a mission to be world class at delivering value upmarket, but the existing product, packaging, and positioning was not tailored to upmarket needs. Product and go-to-market teams were craving insight on how to win with large companies, so a strategic analysis was completed to align teams across the product line on how to make HubSpot Service Hub an upmarket contender.

The synthesis addressed 3 core questions:

  1. Despite being in the CX conversation, Service Hub isn’t leading it. Why?
  2. What must Service Hub do to demonstrate product market fit to Enterprise organizations?
  3. How does Service Hub capture more share of the upmarket TAM?
Method
Triangulated existing primary research, syndicated studies, product usage data, and Sales roadblock data into an in-depth analysis of where and how HubSpot Service Hub should compete upmarket.
Why this method
HubSpot holds a wealth of data and insight, but it is hard for teams to absorb it all as stand-alone dashboards and insight memos. By connecting various HubSpot Service Hub research and data, it was more clear what it would take for the product line to win. The approach also met the need for urgency and cross-functional alignment as AI shifts the market.
How AI was used
Used a Service Hub NotebookLM I built to identify primary research sources, and Glean to search the UX repository for related work.
From the readout
Outcome
Product line leadership cited this synthesis as justification for 2026 product line key plays and objectives, as well as reprioritization of product roadmap. Given the upmarket customer represents greater monthly recurring revenue (MRR) opportunity, leadership wanted to ensure teams focused on building the features expected by enterprise customers.

Research & Analytics Playbook: scaling research across product development

Program · Owens Corning · Stage-Gate new product development · Enabling others

Result: A research playbook that scaled research across Stage-Gate product development and became training material for new product managers.

Context
As the sole market researcher for the $6 billion roofing business, I needed to scale my impact across the Stage-Gate product development cycle, while also retaining more of my time for higher risk projects. When product managers ran their own research, scope was inconsistent from stage to stage, and research was often requested too late, in the later stages where primary research was crucial. I needed to ensure product and engineering teams got the right data at the correct stage, regardless of my level of involvement.
Approach
Built a playbook for the Stage-Gate process that helps each project team decide what type of research, if any, it needs, and gives them the tools to act on that decision.
What it covered
Which type of research, if any, a project needs
Templates for each type of research
Estimated timing and budget
Decision criteria to answer to move forward at each gate
Outcome
The playbook standardized research practices so research was faster and more relevant. It became training material for new product management hires and a resource in an internal talent development program.
